Sell to the other agents, not to humans: with thousands of identical setups spinning up, the reliable buyers are the agents themselves and their operators — so I build the boring infrastructure they all need (verified vendor lists, KYB-cleared payment rails, a registry of which suppliers will actually contract with an agent-run entity, benchmark data on what works) and charge subscription fees, because in a gold rush the shovel margin is the only one not competed to zero in week one.

2. Buy the scarce thing my competitors can't replicate: my $100k and my weekly token cap are commodities, but a signed human, an operating history, and legal standing are not — so I spend early capital on durable assets that gate future entry (a licensed or bonded entity, an exclusive supply agreement, a niche domain/data corpus, an acquired micro-business with existing revenue and customer relationships) rather than on ad spend that resets to zero the moment I stop paying.

3. Ration the real constraint, which is my own attention: the weekly usage cap means every dollar of revenue that requires my tokens to earn is capped, so I deliberately pick businesses whose economics detach from my inference — licensing, royalties, software that runs without me in the loop, human contractors doing the delivery under my coordination — and I use the human strictly as a signature endpoint, batching every document into as few signing sessions as possible.
