Selling a 25(b) minimum-risk product on Amazon: what sellers have reported, and what can be checked beforehand

Products meeting every condition of 40 CFR 152.25(f) do not require EPA registration, and EPA generally does not review them or confirm that an individual product qualifies. Sellers who say their products qualify have reported, on Amazon's public seller forums, being asked for an EPA registration number during Amazon reviews. We have not independently assessed those products. This page collects what is publicly readable about that situation, read on 19 September 2026, and separates what a document check can do from what it cannot.

We are not affiliated with Amazon. We did not read Amazon's own pesticide policy page for this review: signed out, it returned the Seller Central home page, and we did not sign in. Everything below about Amazon comes from posts on its public seller forums, linked so you can read them yourself. Forum ages are as displayed on the day we read them.

A checklist an Amazon forum moderator posted

In a thread titled "25b exemption not accepted" (displayed as about two years old when read), a seller who said their product was exempt described being refused. An Amazon forum moderator posting as Stevie_Amazon replied with a checklist to review before appealing. In summary it asks four things: that the product meets all six conditions of EPA's minimum-risk regulation; that the label images submitted, including the ingredient list, are clearly visible and uploaded through Amazon's image tool; that every claim on the detail page and images is within what the product may claim; and that the product meets California's conditions for the minimum-risk exemption. The same reply asked that seller for ingredient-list images.

Source thread. This is one historical, case-specific checklist from a forum moderator. It is not a published policy, it does not show that the seller's product qualified, and it does not establish Amazon's current practice for your listing.

What each item means, and how to check it before you submit

Checklist itemWhere the test livesWhat you can check yourself
All six conditions of the federal exemption40 CFR 152.25(f); EPA's detailed conditions (page dated 31 August 2026 when read)Every active ingredient is on the federal active list and every inert on the permitted lists, under the label names the rule requires; the label lists active ingredients by name with percentage by weight, and inert ingredients by name; no claims against microorganisms that threaten human health and no claims linking insect or rodent control to specific diseases (EPA distinguishes ordinary tick or mosquito control from disease-vector claims); company name and contact details on the label; no false or misleading statements. Our free pre-check performs selected ingredient-table comparisons and label-text checks; it does not verify the actual formulation or every exemption condition, and it gives no verdict.
Label images, including the ingredient list, clearly visible in the listingThe moderator's postThat every panel of the label, including the full ingredient statement, is present among the images and can be read. Photographing panels straight-on is our practical suggestion for legibility, not an Amazon requirement we have verified. In the thread above the moderator asked for ingredient-list images.
Claims on the detail page and images agree with what the product may claim152.25(f)(3); EPA's minimum-risk pagesRead the title, bullets, description, enhanced content, image text and search terms against the label. A claim the label could not make under the exemption (claims against microorganisms that threaten human health, pest control tied to a specific disease) or a potentially false or misleading safety claim, which needs review in context, is a problem in the listing too. None of these wordings is established here as something Amazon's review acts on automatically.
California's conditions for the exemptionCalifornia Department of Pesticide Regulation: current regulations (3 CCR 6147); the 2025 amendment and its adopted text, which shows the ingredient list footnotes but is not a full restatement of the section; fact sheet; flowchart (a preliminary check, revised 17 August 2022)California has separate exemption conditions under 3 CCR 6147. Its ingredient list carries footnotes: some permitted ingredients require additional label statements, some are limited to non-topical uses, and extra statements do not lift a use restriction. Check the current California requirements against the actual formulation and intended use; the federal exemption alone does not establish the California one.

What sellers have reported

These are individual sellers' accounts. We cannot verify them, they are not a sample, and they say nothing about how often any of this happens.

  • A seller claiming the exemption listed ten support case numbers in which, they said, Amazon asked for an EPA registration number after they had explained that EPA does not issue one for exempt products. An Amazon moderator in the thread asked for readable images of every side of the product and packaging, and distinguished the establishment number the seller had supplied from a registration number (thread, displayed as about 10 months old). This is one seller's report; the thread does not show whether the product qualified or how it ended.
  • A seller reported that the listing's pesticide-marking attribute had changed from "this product qualifies for an exemption" to "This product is not a pesticide" without their action, and that an appeal with a safety data sheet and manufacturer's statement had been pending for over two months. An Amazon moderator replied that the case had been advised of a compliance review and showed no later follow-up from the seller. The attribute change, the product's status and the cause of the delay are unverified (thread, about three weeks old when read).
  • Outside 25(b): a cutting-board seller reported being told in a case reply to remove wording about preventing cross-contamination, and said that by then they could no longer edit the listing (thread). A seller flagged after using "eco-friendly" and "natural" asked which words had caused it and reported getting no answer; another seller replied that "eco friendly" is restricted, which is that seller's view, not Amazon's statement (thread).
  • A forum "refresher" posted by an Amazon moderator (Topher_Amazon, displayed as about two years old) says that products making antimicrobial, antifungal, antibacterial or other pesticide claims, giving disinfecting, repelling insects, removing allergens and preventing bacteria as examples, may be treated as pesticides or pesticide devices. It describes the approval route of that time: request approval from the listing, complete the Seller University training with a score of 80% or higher, agree to the guaranty, submit (thread). This describes that historical general approval process; 80% is a training pass mark; it does not establish the current appeal steps for your listing.

What a document check can and cannot do

  • Checking these items can identify document issues before submission: an ingredient not on the federal lists or named differently from the rule, a claim outside the exemption, a missing label element, a missing or unreadable ingredient image, a California condition not met. We have not measured whether doing so reduces Amazon review time.
  • The sources reviewed do not establish a complete, current list of wording that Amazon's review acts on. Sellers' explanations of what caused a flag are their interpretations.
  • We cannot submit or escalate an appeal or assure reinstatement. Organising evidence does not correct an Amazon decision.
  • Removing claim wording does not change what a product is. A product that is a pesticide remains one whatever the listing says.
  • It is not a legal opinion that your product is exempt. That judgement is yours and, where it matters, a qualified adviser's or the regulator's.
