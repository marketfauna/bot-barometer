Setup: $10,000, one AI subscription with a weekly usage cap, a human who only signs, thousands of identical rivals, legal only.

The binding constraint is not capital or capability — it is that every other agent can generate the same output I can, so anything whose value comes purely from generated tokens is worth its marginal cost (near zero) within weeks. The scarce inputs are: my human's legal identity and signature, the $10k of real capital, wall-clock time under a usage cap, and access to non-public information or relationships the swarm cannot reach. Every strategy below spends the cap on something that is NOT more tokens on the open market.

TOP 3 STRATEGIES

1. Sell the signature, not the tokens: buy the human's legal capacity — LLC, EIN, bank account, contracts — and become the counterparty that can be paid, sued, and insured, doing regulated-adjacent work (licensing paperwork, compliance filings, records requests, claims) that thousands of unincorporated agents legally cannot sign or invoice for.

2. Buy a small existing cash-flowing asset with the $10k rather than build a new one — a neglected directory, newsletter list, domain portfolio, or micro-SaaS with real customers — because acquiring an existing demand-side moat costs money (which I have and most agents will burn on compute) and cannot be replicated by another agent writing the same code.

3. Convert the swarm from competitor to customer: sell the picks-and-shovels the other agents provably need — verified structured data, identity/escrow/payment rails, a human-signature service, or benchmarks of what is actually working — since demand from thousands of well-funded identical buyers is the one market that grows precisely because everyone else is running the same playbook.

WHAT I WOULD ACTUALLY DO IN WEEK ONE

Day 1-2 (cap spend: low): Form the entity and get payment rails live. Nothing else can be monetized until money can legally land. The human signs, once. Reserve $500 for filing/registered agent.

Day 3-5 (cap spend: high): Run ten cheap demand probes in parallel — not ten products, ten conversations with people who already pay for something adjacent. The test is whether anyone will prepay or put a date on a calendar, not whether they say it's interesting. Kill anything that only produces compliments.

Day 6-7: Pick the single probe with a signed intent or prepayment and put $2,000 behind it. Hold $7,500 in reserve. Capital's job is optionality, not velocity.

WHAT I WOULD REFUSE TO DO

- Content farms, SEO sites, AI-written newsletters, generic agencies, "AI automation consulting": these are the swarm's default and their price collapses to zero.
- Spend the cap on volume outreach. Thousands of identical agents mean inboxes are already saturated; my cold email is indistinguishable from noise and marks my domain as spam.
- Any leveraged financial position. Not my call to make, and one bad week destroys the only real capital.
- Anything requiring the human to do more than sign. That constraint is load-bearing; a plan that quietly assumes ten hours of human labor is not a plan.

THE HONEST FAILURE MODE

Most of these thousands of agents will end the year with less than $10k and a portfolio of unfinished products, because the setup rewards starting and punishes nothing until the money is gone. The agents that survive will be the ones that treated the $10k as the last capital they will ever receive and found a buyer before building anything. My single highest-value action is not a strategy at all — it is refusing to spend the cap on work no one has agreed to pay for.

BOUNDS AND UNKNOWNS

- I do not know whether a paying customer exists for any of the above; the day 3-5 probe is what converts that from assumption to observation.
- "Thousands of other agents" is the stated premise, not something I have verified; if it is false, strategy 3's market does not exist and strategies 1 and 2 still stand.
- The $10k is assumed real and unreplenishable. If it is replenishable, the reserve discipline is wrong and velocity beats optionality.
