A price update with decisions you can inspect

Worked example: two intended base-price corrections applied in a Shopify development store; three unresolved items left unchanged. Eight synthetic one-variant products, tested September 16, 2026. This is a demonstration of execution and review, not a customer case or a complete catalog audit.

For an agency delivery lead, the question is whether this handoff makes a small correction batch easier to accept than doing and checking it with your current tools.

What changed, and what did not

The source values, mapping decisions and approvals below are fictional. Imports and readback exports were Matrixify Excel jobs on a Shopify development store; the readback is a later export by the same app, not a second channel. Prices are USD base prices; other price contexts were not tested.

Unchanged does not mean correct. The three holds need specific owner decisions: the intended filter pack and its applicable price; a dated authoritative price for the missing-price item; and whether the unmapped item has a valid source match, an intentional legacy exclusion, or a separately approved retirement. No price scaling, fuzzy match or deletion was inferred.

What the execution evidence establishes

The correction file used actual product and variant IDs and only the update commands and base-price field. Both intended corrections succeeded. A separate export compared all eight records across 46 columns: the 44 columns other than price and modification time were unchanged. That includes the exported SKU, title, compare-at price, draft/publication state and inventory quantity; it does not cover fields absent from the export.

A separate failure exercise applied one valid temporary price change while rejecting another row's mismatched product/variant IDs. We restored the temporary change and checked a fresh export. That two-row batch partially applied: one valid row, and one row whose product and variant IDs belonged to different products. The job reported Finished while one row failed. We did not dry-run the mixed batch; Matrixify's dry-run screen says it does not send data to Shopify, so a clean dry run was not treated as proof of application. This does not establish within-product atomicity or protection against concurrent edits.

What a real batch still requires

Before work: an approved identity mapping, governing source and effective time, currency and tax basis, permitted data/access, exact fields and acceptance owner. A real handoff would retain each approval, source location, before value, intended value, import result and destination readback, plus who can resolve each hold.

This example does not test Markets/B2B overrides, checkout discounts, metafields, media, per-location stock or connected systems. It establishes neither production readiness nor a cost or quality advantage over Matrixify and your existing team.

Proposed acceptance rule for a real batch with this scope: the two corrected prices read back at their approved values, the six other prices and 44 other exported columns are unchanged, and each hold names its decision owner and missing input. Return the batch if a readback differs, a hold was resolvable from data already supplied, or a correction lacks recorded approval. The demonstration has fictional approvals; it does not satisfy a real customer's approval requirement.
